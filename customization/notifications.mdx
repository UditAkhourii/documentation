Well-timed reminders can be the difference between starting a task and losing an hour to distraction. ADHD Focus sends gentle nudges designed to support your routine without adding noise. The goal is helpful prompting, not overwhelm.

## Notification types

ADHD Focus offers five notification types you can mix and match. Toggle only the ones that genuinely help you.

| Notification | When it fires | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Focus Reminder** | A few minutes before a scheduled focus session. | Reduces the transition cost of getting started. |
| **Habit Check-in** | At your chosen habit time if you have not logged it yet. | Keeps streaks alive without requiring you to remember. |
| **Daily Goal Reminder** | Once per day at a time you set. | A single anchor moment to review what matters today. |
| **Reward Alert** | When you earn a reward or unlock an achievement. | Reinforces progress with positive feedback. |
| **Session Break Alert** | When a focus session ends and it is time to rest. | Prevents burnout and supports sustainable work rhythms. |

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Settings">
    Tap your profile icon, then choose **Settings** from the menu.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select Notifications">
    Tap **Notifications** to see every available alert type.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Toggle what you need">
    Turn on only the notifications you want. Tap any active notification to set its time or frequency.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Your changes apply immediately. You can return anytime to adjust.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Enabling too many notifications can cause notification fatigue, where your brain starts ignoring all alerts. If you notice yourself swiping away reminders without acting, turn off the least useful ones and keep only one or two core nudges.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  If you tend to dismiss multiple alerts, try scheduling a single **Daily Goal Reminder** mid-morning. Use that one moment to open ADHD Focus, review your tasks, and decide what to prioritize. One reliable nudge often beats five ignored ones.
</Tip>
